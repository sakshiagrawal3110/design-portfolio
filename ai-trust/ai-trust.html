01 · QUESTION & HYPOTHESIS

AI can give us an answer in seconds. But what makes us believe it? Capability alone doesn't automatically create trust.

01 · THE RESEARCH QUESTION

What makes users trust, question, or reject AI-generated information?

  • Accepting an AI answer without checking it
  • Verifying information they don't fully understand
  • Rejecting an answer even when it is correct
  • Trusting confident language more than actual evidence
  • Feeling uncomfortable when AI reasoning is opaque
02 · RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS

Understandable & controllable over confident

Rather than starting with "Users don't trust AI," I framed a testable hypothesis:

"Trust may not come from making AI appear more confident. It may come from making AI more understandable and controllable."

02 · MIXED-METHODS PLAN

Steps 03–07: A structured framework combining observed interview recall, a 5–7 day diary study, and controlled UI testing.

04 · ARCHETYPE 01

AI Explorers

People who regularly use ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and similar assistants across daily workflows.

04 · ARCHETYPE 02

AI Pragmatists

People who use AI occasionally for specific, bounded tasks when it saves time.

04 · ARCHETYPE 03

AI Skeptics

People who actively question, double-check, or avoid AI-generated information.

05 · INTERVIEW APPROACH · BEHAVIOUR > OPINION

"Tell me about the last time an AI tool gave you an answer you weren't sure about."

Instead of asking "Do you trust AI?", probing specific incidents uncovered observed behaviour: what triggered doubt, where they verified it, and what would have built confidence.

06 · DIARY STUDY · 5–7 DAYS

AI was trusted as a starting point, not as evidence

Trust happens in moments, not surveys. Logging real context, AI outputs, reactions, verification steps, and final decisions revealed how users treat AI drafts in the wild.

07 · USABILITY STUDY · TESTING INTERFACE VARIABLES

Three versions of the same AI response

Participants evaluated the exact same recommendation across three UI treatments while measuring confidence, willingness to act, and perceived transparency:

VERSION A Answer only

Polished recommendation with no supporting rationale or controls.

VERSION B Answer + explanation

Adds rationale behind the output, but lacks verifiable sources or criteria edits.

VERSION C · HIGHEST TRUST Answer + evidence + control

Pairs supporting evidence with transparent parameters the user can inspect and adjust.

03 · SYNTHESIS & INSIGHTS

Steps 08–09: Interview transcripts and diary logs synthesized in FigJam into five core themes—Understanding, Verification, Control, Uncertainty, and Responsibility.

INSIGHT 01

Trust isn't binary

Users readily trust AI for Discovery → Organisation → Summarisation, but hesitate sharply when AI moves toward Recommendation → Decision → Action.

INSIGHT 02

Confidence isn't the same as credibility

Polished, confident language can feel convincing, yet comfort comes from answering: Where did this come from? What information was used? Can I verify it?

INSIGHT 03

Users don't necessarily want more explanation

They want the right explanation at the right moment. Instead of exposing technical AI reasoning, surface useful, scannable evidence.

INSIGHT 04

Control is a trust mechanism

Trust increases when users can Inspect → Modify → Verify → Reject, rather than being forced into a binary Accept → Continue flow.

04 · SPECTRUM & DIRECTION

Steps 10–12: Mapping risk levels to proportional user control so AI assists decisions rather than silently making them.

LOW RISK · ASSISTIVE Discovery → Organisation → Summarisation

Fast synthesis with lightweight source links and easy undo.

→
HIGH RISK · HUMAN-LED Recommendation → Decision → Action

Requires visible evidence, uncertainty flags, adjustable criteria, and explicit approval.

11 · FROM RESEARCH TO PRODUCT

Translating patterns into design principles

Moves product teams from raw research observations to concrete interaction rules: when to explain, when to surface evidence, when to show uncertainty, and when to step back.

12 · CONCEPTUAL DIRECTION

AI should assist the decision, not silently make it

Instead of saying "This is the best option," the UI explains parameters, surfaces supporting evidence, and lets users adjust criteria—helping them evaluate rather than trust blindly.

OUTCOME

A framework for safer AI experiences—turning AI trust from an abstract worry into an actionable product design system.

"The goal isn't to make users trust AI more. It's to help users know when AI deserves their trust."

WHEN TO EXPLAIN Right Moment

Contextual rationale without overwhelming technical jargon.

WHEN TO PROVE Useful Evidence

Clear provenance answering where information came from.

WHEN TO FLAG Visible Uncertainty

Honest cues when data is incomplete or ambiguous.

WHEN TO STEP BACK Human Control

Inspect, modify, verify, or reject on consequential actions.
