QUICK SUMMARY

AI Trust Research - TL;DR

  • Hypothesis: Trust doesn't come from making AI appear more confident-it comes from making AI more understandable and controllable[cite: 23].
  • Mixed-Methods Study: Combined behavioural interviews, a 5–7 day diary study across 12 participants, and a 3-variant usability study (Answer only vs. Answer + explanation vs. Answer + evidence + control)[cite: 23, 31].
  • Core Takeaway: Users trust AI as a starting point (Discovery → Organisation → Summarisation), not as evidence-and confidence rises when they can Inspect → Modify → Verify → Reject[cite: 23, 29].

01 · QUESTION & HYPOTHESIS

AI can give us an answer in seconds. But what makes us believe it? Capability alone doesn't automatically create trust[cite: 23].

01 · THE RESEARCH QUESTION

What makes users trust, question, or reject AI-generated information?[cite: 23]

  • Accepting an AI answer without checking it[cite: 23]
  • Verifying information they don't fully understand[cite: 23]
  • Rejecting an answer even when it is correct[cite: 23]
  • Trusting confident language more than actual evidence[cite: 23]
  • Feeling uncomfortable when AI reasoning is opaque[cite: 23]
02 · RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS

Understandable & controllable over confident[cite: 23]

Rather than starting with "Users don't trust AI," I framed a testable hypothesis[cite: 23]:

"Trust may not come from making AI appear more confident. It may come from making AI more understandable and controllable."[cite: 23]

INSIGHT 01

Trust isn't binary[cite: 29, 30]

Users readily trust AI for Discovery → Organisation → Summarisation, but hesitate sharply when AI moves toward Recommendation → Decision → Action[cite: 23, 29].

INSIGHT 02

Confidence isn't the same as credibility[cite: 29]

Polished, confident language can feel convincing, yet comfort comes from answering: Where did this come from? What information was used? Can I verify it?[cite: 23, 29]

INSIGHT 03

Users don't necessarily want more explanation[cite: 23]

They want the right explanation at the right moment. Instead of exposing technical AI reasoning, surface useful, scannable evidence[cite: 23, 25].

INSIGHT 04

Control is a trust mechanism[cite: 23, 29]

Trust increases when users can Inspect → Modify → Verify → Reject, rather than being forced into a binary Accept → Continue flow[cite: 23, 29].

OUTCOME

A framework for safer AI experiences-turning AI trust from an abstract worry into an actionable product design system[cite: 23, 25].

"The goal isn't to make users trust AI more. It's to help users know when AI deserves their trust."[cite: 23, 25]

WHEN TO EXPLAIN Right Moment

Contextual rationale without overwhelming technical jargon[cite: 23, 25].

WHEN TO PROVE Useful Evidence

Clear provenance answering where information came from[cite: 23, 25].

WHEN TO FLAG Visible Uncertainty

Honest cues when data is incomplete or ambiguous[cite: 23, 25].

WHEN TO STEP BACK Human Control

Inspect, modify, verify, or reject on consequential actions[cite: 23, 25].
